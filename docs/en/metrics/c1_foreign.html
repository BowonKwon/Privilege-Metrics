Foreign links

21.6%England 1840–1852
Key point

The share of English grants with a foreign link ('communication' or an address abroad) is 2.5% in the 18th century and 21.6% in 1840–1852. Hulme counts 21 of 55 grants of 1561–1603 (four of them grants for regulating trade) as grants to aliens. In the Dutch Republic 18% of privilege documents of 1581–1621 name a grantee of known foreign origin, 23% if grantees of probable foreign extraction are included.

Definition
England 1617–1852: share of grants whose text says 'communication' (an invention communicated from abroad) or whose grantee lives outside the British Isles. 1561–1603: Hulme's alien count and our recoding from his notes. Dutch Republic: origin code in the Buning data.
Formula
grants with a foreign link ÷ grants, by decade; 95% Wilson interval
How to read it
In England 'communication' entries grow from the 1810s; by the 1840s–50s about one grant in five has a foreign link.
Caveats
Each jurisdiction defines 'foreign' differently. The English figure is a lower bound read from addresses and wording; nationality is not recorded. Our recoding from Hulme's notes finds fewer aliens than Hulme's own 21. For the Dutch Republic, Buning's codes 1–6 (a named foreign origin) count as foreign and code 7 (probably of foreign extraction) is shown separately; codes 8 and 9 mark Dutch grantees.

Prior results

  • 55 grants 1561–1603: 21 to aliens, 30 to natives, 4 for regulating trade. — Hulme (1900), LQR 16, p. 52.
  • 6.5% of the 169 patents of 1474–1550 went to foreign (non-Italian) inventors. — Comino, Galasso & Graziano (2017), pp. 21–22.
