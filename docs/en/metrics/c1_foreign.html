Foreign links

22.1%England 1840–1852
Key point

The share of English grants with a foreign link ('communication' or an address abroad) is 3.7% in the 18th century and 22.1% in 1840–1852. Hulme counts 21 of 55 grants of 1561–1603 as grants to aliens. In the Dutch Republic 41% of privilege documents of 1581–1621 name a grantee of foreign origin.

Definition
England 1617–1852: share of grants whose text says 'communication' (an invention communicated from abroad) or whose grantee lives outside the British Isles. 1561–1603: Hulme's alien count and our recoding from his notes. Dutch Republic: origin code in the Buning data.
Formula
grants with a foreign link ÷ grants, by decade; 95% Wilson interval
How to read it
In England 'communication' entries grow from the 1810s; by the 1840s–50s about one grant in five has a foreign link.
Caveats
Each jurisdiction defines 'foreign' differently. The English figure is a lower bound read from addresses and wording; nationality is not recorded. Our recoding from Hulme's notes finds fewer aliens than Hulme's own 21.

Prior results

  • 55 grants 1561–1603: 21 to aliens, 30 to natives, 4 for regulating trade. — Hulme (1900), LQR 16, p. 52.
  • 6.5% of the 169 patents of 1474–1550 went to foreign (non-Italian) inventors. — Comino, Galasso & Graziano (2017), pp. 21–22.
