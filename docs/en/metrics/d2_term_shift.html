D · On what terms

From 21 years to 14

327 / 33214 years or less, 1625–1700
Key point

Of the 332 stated terms in 1625–1700, 327 are 14 years or less; 4 are still 21 years (1631, 1632, 1638, 1663).

Definition
Grants per year 1617–1700 whose first written term is 21 years, and whose first written term is 14 years or less.
Formula
counts per year
How to read it
After 1624 terms of 14 years or less become the rule, but a few 21-year terms remain.
Caveats
Section 6 of the Statute of Monopolies limited patents for new inventions to 14 years or less. The table only counts the terms written in the record. Where an entry states further terms (mines, Ireland, a later revenue) or re-grants the residue of an earlier term (column term_other in the entry table, counted per year in this table), the first term is counted.

Names: Hulme and Woodcroft compiled lists of English monopoly grants and patents (1561–1603 and 1617–1852); Fulin edited documents on Venetian printing (1469–1526); Buning's data cover Dutch patents (1581–1621); SRO is Stationers' Register Online, a transcription of the register of the London Stationers' Company. References for the sources used on this page are under Sources below; those for all five are on the method page.

A figure such as 119/120 means that two values agree on 119 of the 120 entries checked. What is compared with what is in the row name: the rows on image checks, transcription, recoding and field agreement compare the site's value with the value another reader found or assigned from the page image or the text. 95% CI (95% confidence interval, Wilson method) is a range for the true rate of agreement estimated from the sample: intervals made this way from repeated samples would contain the true value about 95 times in 100. Other things being similar, the smaller the sample, the wider it is. Recoding is another person reading the same entry and assigning its categories again. 'Communication' marks a grant whose text says the invention was communicated from abroad.
