B · Which trades

Field composition

Textiles, dyeing and leatherLargest field 1840–1852
Key point

The largest fields are water raising, drainage and hydraulics, metals and metalworking, textiles, dyeing and leather in 1617–1649, and textiles, dyeing and leather, carriages, railways, ships and navigation, steam and motive power in 1840–1852. The unmatched 'other' share ranges from 7% to 20% by period.

Definition
Share of granted inventions by field. The field is the first match of a pre-registered list of word rules applied to the title of the invention (the method page describes the steps that remove driving-power and added-use phrases and match object names first).
Formula
count in field and period ÷ count in period (including 'other')
How to read it
In 1617–1649 water raising and drainage and metals take large shares; after 1820, textiles, transport and steam. Agreement with the coder is uneven across fields, from 45% to 100% leaving 'other' aside (10 of 25 for 'other'; see the reliability card).
Caveats
Classification uses the words of the title, so an invention that spans two fields goes to the one earlier in the list. 'Other' is what no rule matched. The rules agree with an independent coder reading the text on about three entries in four (precision weighted by field shares 75%; see the reliability card), so read the shares as a rough composition and do not weigh small changes in a single field. For Hulme 1561–1603 the same rules were applied to the subject line.
