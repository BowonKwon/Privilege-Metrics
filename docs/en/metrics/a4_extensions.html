A · How many

Share of extension entries

36Extension entries
Key point

Extension entries number 36, 0.25% of all entries; the decade with most is the 1840s (13).

Definition
Share of Woodcroft's numbered entries that grant an extension or confirmation of an earlier patent, by decade.
Formula
extension entries ÷ entries
How to read it
Extensions are rare: under 1% of all entries.
Caveats
Counts entries whose text contains 'extension', 'prolongation' or 'confirmation'. The 6 numbers whose text the OCR lost cannot be checked; they stay in the denominator as entries that are not extensions, so the share is a lower bound (0.29% of all entries even if all 6 were extensions).

Names: Hulme and Woodcroft compiled lists of English monopoly grants and patents (1561–1603 and 1617–1852); Fulin edited documents on Venetian printing (1469–1526); Buning's data cover Dutch patents (1581–1621); SRO is Stationers' Register Online, a transcription of the register of the London Stationers' Company. References for the sources used on this page are under Sources below; those for all five are on the method page.

A figure such as 119/120 means that two values agree on 119 of the 120 entries checked. What is compared with what is in the row name: the rows on image checks, transcription, recoding and field agreement compare the site's value with the value another reader found or assigned from the page image or the text. 95% CI (95% confidence interval, Wilson method) is a range for the true rate of agreement estimated from the sample: intervals made this way from repeated samples would contain the true value about 95 times in 100. Other things being similar, the smaller the sample, the wider it is. 'Communication' marks a grant whose text says the invention was communicated from abroad.
