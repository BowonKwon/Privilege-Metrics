Method and limits

What was counted

English invention privileges and patents come from two lists: Hulme (1896, 1900), who drew 55 industrial monopoly grants of 1561–1603 from the calendars of the Patent Rolls, and Woodcroft (1854), whose chronological list numbers 1 to 14,359 for 1617–1852. 1604–1616 falls between the two lists. Hulme (1896) notes that a return of grants made before 1623, ordered by the Commons in 1827, was never presented.

The Stationers' Register is read straight from the Stationers' Register Online TEI (1557–1640, 10,453 entries). Venetian printing privileges are Fulin's 256 documents (1882), read and coded one by one. For 14 of those coded as privileges, Fulin's summary records the petition but not the grant; they were counted on the strength of the penalty clause or of editions 'con privilegio' cited in Fulin's notes, and the note column says so. The Dutch figures are a recount of the data published by Buning and Looijesteijn.

Venetian invention patents were not counted here. The record-level source checked here is Berveglieri's printed calendar (2020); earlier, Mandich (1936, 1948) transcribed and classified 109 grants of 1474–1550 (Comino et al. 2017). Figures from the literature are marked 'secondary' and kept apart.

Reading Woodcroft's list

The word coordinates of the Internet Archive OCR were used to separate the margin column (surnames, sealing dates) from the text column (numbers and abstracts). Because numbers must run from 1 to 14,359, the longest rising chain of number-like lines was kept and candidates caught in its gaps were renumbered in order. 115 pages on which the OCR lost entry numbers were read again from the page images with tesseract 5. 6 numbers still have no text (nos. 4790, 6964, 12304, 13365, 13366 and 13809; they can be read on the page images, but the program did not split them off): they are counted in the yearly totals (with their neighbors' year) and left out of metrics that need the text, except the share of extension entries, which keeps them in the denominator as entries that are not extensions and is therefore a lower bound.

Dates are the sealing dates printed in the margin. For 13,638 entries the margin date is used as read. Margin dates damaged by the OCR (354 entries) are used only when their year falls between the clean dates before and after, and the day is kept only when its digits are clean. For 15 entries whose margin date the OCR lost altogether, the date was read on the page image (data/coding/woodcroft_date_overrides.csv). Otherwise the neighbors' year was used when both neighbors agreed (352 entries).

Version 1.0.7 corrected two defects in the reading program. Two regular expressions held a control character (U+0008) where a word boundary was meant. One is the step that splits off entries whose number was lost by the words 'A grant unto'; it never worked from 1.0 to 1.0.6. After the correction the numbers without text fell from 36 to 6, the grants rose from 14,287 to 14,317, and 26 entries that had carried the next entry in their text were separated. The other is the step that cuts the invention text at 'to hold'. Because it did not cut, the wording of the enrolment clause ('instrument in writing' and the like) entered the field classification, and 24 grants had a different field. This second defect was found by independent verification. The numbers of the 56 entries whose text changed are in coding/reliability/reparse_1.0.7.csv.

A year that differs from both neighbors while they agree with each other is treated as an OCR misreading. Nos. 7046 and 7049 are printed '29th March 1835' in Woodcroft itself among entries of March 1836; they are taken as misprints and counted in 1836.

A margin line containing digits is a date, not a grantee's surname: the number of grantees (shared grants) counts only margin lines without digits. An address abroad is a place outside the British Isles in the grantee's description; London street names such as 'Hanover Square' and memberships of foreign societies do not count.

The term is the first term stated in the abstract, read in early-modern spellings (fourteene, one and twentie), Roman numerals (xiiij, xxjtie) and OCR-damaged forms (xilij, 14^years). Where an entry states further terms (mines, Ireland, a later revenue), they are kept in the column term_other, and a grant of the residue of an earlier term is marked 'residue' there. In 1617–1700, 7 entries state a further or residual term; of these, 1 is a re-grant of the residue of an earlier patent (residue). The column is read after the first term is fixed and never changes it. For nos. 1–126 (1617–1640) the first term agrees in every case with an independent reading of the page images. From about 1820 the abstracts stop stating the term, so later terms are left blank; the statutory 14 years was not assumed. Version 1.0.8 reads the abbreviation 'yrs'. Abstracts of about 1785–1815 write 'for the term of 14 yrs', and the rule until then read only 'years' and its old spellings, leaving these terms blank. OCR-damaged forms (14 yls, 14 y™) are read only directly after 'term of' and the digits. The short form of about 1815 ('to hold, &c, 14 years') and forms in which 'of' is lost or damaged ('for the term 14 yrs') are read as well. 248 terms were added, all blank when the earlier rule is applied to the same text; 247 are 14 years. Against the published 1.0.5 table the entries with a stated term rise by 251: of the 248, no. 2873 was already read in 1.0.5, and four entries whose text was recovered by the re-read of 1.0.7 (nos. 2628, 2874, 3067 and 3530) are added. Damaged forms are read only as they actually occur in the list ('term of 14 yds' and the like are not read). The remaining one, no. 3783 (1814), is printed 'for the term of 41 yrs pursuant to the statute' in Woodcroft; it is taken as a misprint of the statutory 14 and counted as 14. The 14 in the table is this site's judgment, not the figure printed in Woodcroft, and it was not checked against the enrolled patent. The defect was found by independent verification, which checked the blanks of nos. 2873 and 1986 against the page.

Rents, provisos and foreign links are judged from the words of the abstract. The rent metric is the share of entries with rent wording, not a legal finding that rent was owed.

Field classification

There are 17 fields. Word rules are applied to the title of the invention in a fixed order and the first match is taken. Entries before 1700 do not set the title off in quotation marks, so the whole abstract is used, after early-modern spellings are normalized.

The rules were fixed before any results were seen but were revised three times before publication. r2 removed the bare word 'instrument' (any device, in 17th-century usage) from the instruments field; r3 added the spelling table and widened the text used for entries before 1700. These two followed an implausible first result.

Checked against an independent coder's recoding of 212 entries from the text (R2), the r3 rules agreed on 144/212 (68%, 95% CI 61–74%) and on only 2 of 11 mining entries: 'tan pits', 'pit coal' and 'smelting copper ore' had gone to mining and gas lighting to chemicals. After the r4 fix, precision was measured again on a fresh sample of 212 entries drawn with another seed (R2b), not on the sample that prompted the fix. The revisions are recorded at the head of src/fields.py.

After publication, version 1.0.3 revised the rules once more (r5), prompted by the R2b disagreements and by two misclassifications found in independent verification (no. 5999, chimneys for houses, went to metals because of the grantee's name 'Smith'; no. 2999, water pipes laid under ground, went to 'other'). Unquoted titles from 1700 are now classified without the grantee's name, address and trade; water raising and steam come before mining (a pump for mines is classified as a pump); and the word rules for calico printing, 'powder sugar', corn, locks and chimneys were adjusted. On R2b the r4 rules had agreed on 145/212 (68%, 95% CI 62–74%) (κ 0.67, 67% weighted). r5 was measured not on R2b but on a fresh sample of 212 entries drawn with a new seed, entries of R2 and R2b excluded (R2c), coded for field by another coder from the text alone: 157/212 (74%, 95% CI 68–79%) (κ 0.73, 72% weighted). Of the 25 entries R2c's rules left as 'other', the coder also put only 8 there; no. 692 (embossing, printing or staining woollen goods) went to printing, no. 8664 (looms worked by hand, steam, water or other power) to steam, and no. 1020 (continued tones from the wire strings of a harpsichord) to metals, while both coders put them under textiles, textiles and instruments: the order of the word rules alone did not find the object of the invention.

Version 1.0.6 revised the rules again (r6) from the R2c disagreements. The principle is the coders' rule: the field is the object of the invention, not the power that drives it, its material or where it is used. A phrase that only names the driving power ('worked by hand, steam, water or any other power') is removed before matching; only if nothing then matches is the full text used, so 'an engine worked by the power of steam' stays under steam. Names of objects such as musical instruments, clocks, looms and lamps are matched before the other word rules, so 'wire strings' or 'glass lamps' no longer send an entry to metals or glass. Printing or staining woollen and worsted goods goes to textiles, not printing, and the old spelling 'dying' is read as dyeing. Every entry from 1700 whose title falls to 'other' is classified again on the words of the invention after the grantee, up to 'to hold'. The rule was written for titles that kept only the term clause, but it applies to all 'other' titles from 1700, not to those alone. A few words were added for water, steam, transport, metals, printing and fuel (head of src/fields.py). Of the 14,287 grants of 1.0.6, 595 changed field, and 'other' fell from 2,806 (19.6%) to 2,508 (17.6%). R2c was used to write r6, so it is not a measurement of r6.

r6 was measured on a fresh sample of 212 entries drawn with a new seed, entries of R2, R2b and R2c excluded (R2d): 152/212 (72%, 95% CI 65–77%) (κ 0.70, 72% weighted); r5 on the same entries gives 149/212 (70%, 95% CI 64–76%) (κ 0.69, 69% weighted). Each weighted figure groups the entries by that dictionary's fields and uses that dictionary's field shares. Only 3 entries of R2d differ between r5 and r6, so this comparison can show little. A separate simple random sample of 60 grants whose field changed (R2d-chg) was therefore coded by both coders, who agree on 56/60 (93%, 95% CI 84–97%) (κ 0.92). Of the 56 entries on which they agree, only r6 is right on 37/56 (66%, 95% CI 53–77%), only r5 on 8/56 (14%, 95% CI 7–26%), and neither on 11/56 (20%, 95% CI 11–32%). In this sample of changed entries, among those on which both coders agree, improvements outnumber regressions. There are new errors too: ships propelled by steam go to transport once the power phrase is removed, while the coders put them under steam, which comes earlier in the list; early entries whose title kept only the term clause are often still misclassified from the substitute text. In R2d the coder also put 4/25 of the 25 'other' entries under 'other', and building and mining agree on only 4 of 11.

All 141 grants that the fallback rule of 1.0.6 moved out of 'other' were coded separately from the text by an outside verifier (R2e). The test was fixed before the coding: the rule stays if it is right more often than 'other' was, and if the lower bound of the 95% interval of its share right is at least 50%. The field the rule chose is right on 86/141 (61%, 95% CI 53–69%), 'other' was right on 9, and neither on 46. The rule passes and stays. But one moved entry in three went to a wrong field, and for 1700–1749 alone the rule is right on 27/53 (51%, 95% CI 38–64%). The fall of the 'other' share in that period from the 24.0% of 1.0.5 means that fewer entries are left unmatched, not that as many are now classified correctly. Of the 46, 16 had gone to printing and 6 to arms; some of these come from the defect corrected in 1.0.7 (the invention text was not cut at 'to hold', so the wording of the enrolment clause entered the classification). R2e measures the 1.0.6 classification as it stood before that correction.

Version 1.0.7 revised the rules again (r7) to settle the boundaries shown by the R2d-chg regressions and by independent verification. The principle: the object comes first, and the rule 'first field in the list' is used only when the object itself belongs to two fields. If what is improved is the engine, the field is steam; if it is the ship, the carriage or its propulsion, the field is transport. Names of objects such as musical instruments, clocks, looms and lamps are matched first only when they are the head of the title; a name that follows a use phrase ('for …ing', 'applicable to', 'such as') is skipped (no. 2301, a centrifugal engine for raising water with 'clock work' among its uses; no. 12421, machinery for figuring fabrics 'applicable to … musical instruments'). A clause that adds further uses ('applicable to …') is removed before matching, like a driving-power phrase, and the full text is used only if nothing then matches. 'Iron mine', a period word for ironstone, no longer counts as mining (no. 502). Locomotive engines stay under steam and vessels propelled by steam under transport. When two object names are both heads of the title, the field earlier in the list is taken. The rules remain word rules, however, and do not guarantee the principle. On test titles written by the independent verifier, 'paddle wheels for steam boats' went to steam ('steam' comes before transport in the list), 'a nautical instrument for measuring the way of a ship' to transport, and 'a machine for making wheels for clocks' to transport as well. These limits follow from the fixed order of the fields and are left for the next revision. On the re-read text, 115 of the 14,317 grants differ between r6 and r7, and 'other' is 2,535 (17.7%).

r7 was measured on a fresh sample of 212 entries drawn with a new seed, all entries of the earlier samples (R2, R2b, R2c, R2d, R2d-chg, R2e) excluded (R2f). The coders' instructions were rewritten with the principle above and boundary examples, so the R2f figures cannot be set side by side with those of earlier versions, coded under the old instructions. Agreement is 163/212 (77%, 95% CI 71–82%) (κ 0.75, 75% weighted); r6, applied to the re-read text, gives 160/212 (75%, 95% CI 69–81%) on the same entries (κ 0.74, 73% weighted). 3 entries of R2f differ between r6 and r7. A separate simple random sample of 60 grants whose field changed (R2f-chg) was coded by both coders, who agree on 56/60 (93%, 95% CI 84–97%) (κ 0.92). Of the 56 entries on which they agree, only r7 is right on 37/56 (66%, 95% CI 53–77%), only r6 on 11/56 (20%, 95% CI 11–32%), and neither on 8/56 (14%, 95% CI 7–26%). The regressions include entries that left instruments because a clock name after a use phrase is now skipped (no. 872, a tool for deepening clock and watch wheels; no. 9993, machinery for making parts of watches) and entries where, once the added-use clause was removed, a remaining word matched another field (nos. 1426 and 12087). In R2f the coder also put 10/25 of the 25 'other' entries under 'other', and the fields with low agreement are mining 5/11; printing_paper 6/11. For R2d, R2d-chg, R2f and R2f-chg the seed and the sampling script were committed to the working repository before the draw; the public repository is exported as one commit per version, so that order does not appear in the public history.

Reliability cards

Image check (R1): an independent coder read 120 entries, 30 per period, from the page images (number, date, number of grantees, 'communication', term) for comparison with the extracted values. Year agreement 119/120 (99%, 95% CI 95–100%), full date 112/120 (93%, 95% CI 87–97%), term 119/120 (99%, 95% CI 95–100%), number of grantees 118/120 (98%, 95% CI 94–100%), communication 119/120 (99%, 95% CI 95–100%).

Text recoding: another coder coded 212 entries (11 per field, 25 'other') from the text alone. Field, under dictionary r7, was measured on R2f: agreement 163/212 (77%, 95% CI 71–82%) (κ 0.75; the sample takes equal numbers per field, so per-field precision weighted by the field shares of the 14,317 grants (extensions excluded, 'other' included) gives 75%). The R2d figure for dictionary r6, 152/212 (72%, 95% CI 65–77%) (72% weighted), and the R2c figure for r5, 157/212 (74%, 95% CI 68–79%) (72% weighted), are kept as records only, since each sample was used to write the next dictionary. Address abroad, communication and term were measured on R2b: address abroad 206/212 (97%, 95% CI 94–99%), communication 208/212 (98%, 95% CI 95–99%), term 212/212 (100%, 95% CI 98–100%).

Fulin recoding (R3): a second coder recoded 40 random documents under the same codebook. Agreement on privilege or not 40/40 (100%, 95% CI 91–100%), term 40/40 (100%, 95% CI 91–100%), beneficiary type 37/40 (92%, 95% CI 80–97%).

Hulme's 55 grants: another coder read the page images independently; dates agree 55/55 (100%, 95% CI 93–100%) and terms 55/55 (100%, 95% CI 93–100%). The decade counts match Hulme's own table on p. 52 (23, 12, 14, 6, 0).

Checks against prior figures

Woodcroft's numbered entries come to 2,239 for 1660–1799 against MacLeod's (1988) 2,240 (a difference of 1), and 13,070 for 1700–1850 against the 13,070 of Nuvolari et al. (2021) (a difference of 0). The counting rules of those studies (whether extensions are included, for instance) were not checked, so the source of the differences is not settled.

The decade totals of the Stationers' Register match the counts returned by the SRO website's search API (2026-11-03; data/sro/sro_site_decade_counts.csv) in every decade. The Fulin count of 240 privileges for 1492–1526 is 14 below Kostylo's (2010) 254, possibly because of what each counts as a privilege.

What this site does not do

It makes no causal estimates. Years such as 1474, 1624 and 1710 are reference points for before-and-after comparison only; the site does not say that a law increased patenting.

Record-level counts of Venetian invention patents, French royal privileges, the Stationers' Register after 1640 and United States federal patents (from 1790) are candidates for later versions.
